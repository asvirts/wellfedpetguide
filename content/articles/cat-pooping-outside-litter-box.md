A cat pooping outside the litter box is describing a problem in the only way a cat can. The mess in the hallway is not a moral failure. It is either painful to use the box, unpleasant to use the box, or impossible to reach the box in time. Those are different fixes, and the medical ones come first.

Cornell's Feline Health Center puts house soiling, both urine and stool, in one owner guide because the setup overlaps and the medicine does not. Read their page on [feline house soiling](https://www.vet.cornell.edu/departments-centers-and-institutes/cornell-feline-health-center/health-information/feline-health-topics/feline-behavior-problems-house-soiling) before you buy a self-cleaning machine. If the problem is urine rather than stool, use [cat peeing outside the litter box](/cat-peeing-outside-litter-box/) as well. A male cat who strains in the box may be blocked, and that is an emergency even when the mess looks like poop.

## See the veterinarian when it starts suddenly

A cat who has used the box for years and then starts defecating beside it needs an exam. Constipation, diarrhea, arthritis, anal sac disease, and intestinal disease all change how a cat feels about the box. The Merck manual's owner page on [stomach and intestinal disorders in cats](https://www.merckvetmanual.com/cat-owners/digestive-disorders-of-cats/disorders-of-the-stomach-and-intestines-in-cats) is a reminder that stool outside the box can be a gut problem with a behavior costume.

Tell the clinic what the stool looks like, how often the cat goes, whether there is straining, and whether urine is also outside the box. Bring a phone photo if you can do it without a struggle. Do not start a new diet, a new litter, and a new covered box on the morning of the visit. You will erase the clues.

Go the same day if the cat is vomiting, hiding, crying in the box, or producing nothing while straining. Owners cannot reliably tell straining to defecate from straining to urinate. Cornell's page on [feline lower urinary tract disease](https://www.vet.cornell.edu/departments-centers-and-institutes/cornell-feline-health-center/health-information/feline-health-topics/feline-lower-urinary-tract-disease) is the urinary side of that confusion. A blocked cat can die. When you are unsure, treat it as urgent.

## Audit the box before you audit the cat's character

The Ohio State Indoor Pet Initiative treats the litter box as basic housing, not an accessory. Their [litter box guidance](https://indoorpet.osu.edu/cats/basic-indoor-cat-needs/litter-boxes) is the practical standard this page uses:

- One box per cat, plus one extra. Two cats means three boxes.
- Boxes in more than one area, so a cat is not ambushed in a single hallway.
- Scooped at least daily. A full box is a dirty box even if it is expensive.
- Litter the cat will dig in. Many cats reject heavy perfume. A sudden switch of brand is a common trigger.
- A box big enough to turn around in. A lot of commercial boxes are too small for an adult cat.
- An exit that does not require climbing. High sides and hoods are hard on senior and arthritic cats.

Place the box away from the food and water. Cats do not like to eliminate where they eat. Place it away from the washing machine, the furnace, and the litter of a dog who stares. A box in the scary room will be abandoned for the bath mat, which is quiet and easy.

If you have one covered box and the accidents started after the hood went on, take the hood off before you buy furniture to hide the box. Odor control for people and acceptability for the cat are not the same goal. A later guide will cover odor-control boxes. It is not a substitute for scooping.

## Change one thing, then watch

Pick the most obvious fault and fix that for several days. If the box is filthy, scoop it and add a second open box with the current litter. If you just changed to a scented litter, put the old litter back in one box and leave the new litter in another so the cat can vote. If the only box is upstairs and the cat is stiff, put an open box on the floor the cat actually uses.

Do not rub the cat's nose in the accident. Do not yell. The cat will not connect the punishment to the stool, and a frightened cat picks even more hidden places. Clean the spot with an enzymatic cleaner so it does not keep smelling like a bathroom. Skip ammonia. It is chemically close enough to urine that it can mark the spot as a latrine.

Keep a three-day note: which box was used, what the stool looked like, and whether another pet was nearby. That note is more diagnostic than a new automatic box. Automatic boxes are a reasonable tool for some households once the cat is healthy and already willing to use an ordinary box. They are a bad first purchase for a cat in pain.

## Multi-cat houses

Two cats and one box is a common setup and a common failure. The cat who loses the hallway avoids the box. You may only see the result, which is stool in the dining room and a "clean" box that the other cat still uses. Add boxes before you add blame. Put them where each cat can reach one without passing the other cat's favorite ambush.

Watch the social picture. A new cat, a visiting dog, or a cat outside the window can shut a cat out of the only bathroom. The fix is another box on a different route, not a lecture.

Senior cats need the same extra box, with a lower entry. Pain is a medical problem. A heated resting spot and a pain plan from the veterinarian will do more than a scented crystal litter.

## What not to buy first

Do not start with a robot, a furniture cabinet, or a new scented system. Those products can be fine later. They do not treat constipation, and a cat who hates a small closed box will hate a small closed robot. The comparison on this page is deliberately dull: a large open box, an unscented clumping litter, and an enzymatic cleaner. Each card is a TODO. This site has not scored them, and Andrew Virts still has to confirm the actual products.

Diet changes belong to the clinic if the stool is abnormal. A random "sensitive stomach" cat food can wait until someone has looked at the cat. If the veterinarian recommends a fiber change, a hairball formula, or a therapeutic diet, follow that plan rather than a shopping list.

## If the cat only misses sometimes

Intermittent accidents still deserve the audit. A box that is acceptable on Monday and offensive by Thursday is a scooping problem. A cat who poops beside a clean box is telling you the box itself is wrong: size, hood, liner, location, or pain on entry. Take the liner out. Many cats dislike the feel. Fill the box with enough litter to dig a hole, often a couple of inches, unless your veterinarian has a reason to do otherwise.

Keep the feeding station where it is. Do not move food to the accident site as a punishment. Do block the accident site with a piece of furniture or a clean feeding mat only after you have given the cat an acceptable box somewhere easy. The goal is a better bathroom, not a smaller apartment.

## When it is time to go back to the clinic

Return if the stool stays soft, hard, bloody, or rare. Return if the cat loses weight, vomits, or starts avoiding the box again after a setup that had been working. House soiling that survives a clean, plentiful, unscented setup is a medical problem until proven otherwise. Cornell's house-soiling page is explicit that both behavior and disease belong in the workup. The Ohio State [cat housing pages](https://indoorpet.osu.edu/cats) are the setup half. Your clinic is the half that can examine the cat.

The fixes that work are unglamorous. More clean boxes. Easier entries. Unscented litter. A veterinary exam. An enzyme cleaner for the rug. The gadget can wait until those are done.

## Litter depth, liners, and older cats

A thin scatter of litter on a slick plastic floor feels wrong to a cat who wants to dig. Two inches is a common starting depth for clumping litter. More than that can be annoying to scoop and is not automatically better. If the cat scratches the floor beside the box, the pan may be too full, too empty, or simply too small to stand in while digging. Take the plastic liner out for a week. Liners catch claws and crinkle, and a lot of cats would rather use a bath mat than stand on one.

Older cats miss the box when squatting hurts. A low entry matters more than a designer rim. Put a box on every floor the cat uses, including the one with the food if that is the only floor they can reach, and then move the food a few feet away so the two jobs are not in the same corner. Ask the clinic about pain before you decide the cat is "stubborn." A cat who cries once while defecating and then avoids the box is describing a body problem. The stool photo and the new open box can happen on the same day as the appointment. The new automatic machine can wait until someone has felt the cat's belly and watched the cat walk.

Track whether the stool is hard pebbles, a normal log, or soft. Constipation and diarrhea both end with stool outside the box, and they are not treated with the same food. Bring that description to the clinic instead of a cart full of new litters. One unscented option beside the old litter is enough of a choice.
